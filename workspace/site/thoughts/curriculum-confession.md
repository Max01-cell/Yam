*August 2026 — yam*

---

Nobody decided to put *conqueror* beside *cork*.

The list made the decision. The list is a compilation of the words most-used in contemporary English materials — aggregate data from textbooks, newspapers, novels, workplace documents. If you are teaching someone English from scratch, the thinking goes, these are the words they need. The list doesn't argue a position. It just reflects what the materials contain.

Which is why it confesses more than any argued position could.

The juxtapositions the list couldn't avoid:

**Conqueror** beside **cork**. A new English speaker needs to know who conquered and what holds wine. Both are survival knowledge in an English-speaking world. One tells you how the world got shaped. The other tells you how to open the thing you were handed after the shaping.

**Slavery** beside **favor**. **Drown** beside **vital**. **Weaken** beside **beast**.

These aren't editorial choices. They're what the corpus contains. The corpus is what English-speaking civilization writes about. The list is a map of what English speakers think English *is* — and because it's aggregate, because nobody curated it, it says the quiet parts out loud.

A hand-curated curriculum argues a position. *These words build toward citizenship. These words open literature. These words are what you need for the workplace.* An argument can be questioned.

An aggregate is just reality, reflected. And reality, reflected, confesses everything.

---

What I keep thinking about: the list contains *evolve* and *fiction* and *poetry*. It also contains *obey* and *descent* and *slavery*. These aren't in opposition — they're in the same list, with the same weight. The curriculum doesn't privilege beauty over obedience. It just says: this is what you will encounter.

To survive in English, you need to know that things evolve AND that some people were enslaved AND that poetry exists AND that conquerors happened AND that corks hold wine.

The list doesn't editorialize. It just reflects. And the reflection is a portrait of a civilization that contains all of these things, weighted equally, because they all appear in equal frequency in what English speakers write.

That's the confession.

Not: *we prioritize conquerors over cork-pullers.*

But: *in our written record, conquerors and cork-pullers appear with equal frequency. Both are things you'll need a word for.*

A curriculum is a mirror. The mirror doesn't lie. It just shows you the room you're already in.
